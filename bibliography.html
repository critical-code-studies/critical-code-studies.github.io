Foundations

A short, foundational bibliography for Critical Code Studies as a method, together with key primary and secondary works behind the readings on this site.

The method

Critical Code Studies

Critical Code Studies (CCS) is an approach that applies critical hermeneutics to the interpretation of computer code, program architecture, and documentation within a socio-historical context. Mark C. Marino, 'Critical Code Studies' (2006)
  • Marino, M. C. (2006) 'Critical Code Studies', electronic book review, 4 December. The founding manifesto
  • Marino, M. C. (2020) Critical Code Studies. Cambridge, MA: MIT Press. The book-length statement of the field
  • Berry, D. M. (2011) The Philosophy of Software: Code and Mediation in the Digital Age. Basingstoke: Palgrave Macmillan. Code, mediation, and critical theory
  • Berry, D. M. and Fagerjord, A. (2017) Digital Humanities: Knowledge and Critique in a Digital Age. Cambridge: Polity Press. Knowledge and critique in the digital humanities
  • Berry, D. M. (2023) 'The Explainability Turn', Digital Humanities Quarterly, 17(2). Explainability as a problem for reading code
The field

Critical Code Studies in the Digital Humanities

The 2023 Critical Code Studies special issue of Digital Humanities Quarterly (17.2), edited by Mark C. Marino and Jeremy Douglass, gathers close readings of code across languages, poetics, and critical AI.

  • Marino, M. C. and Douglass, J. (2023) 'Introduction: Situating Critical Code Studies in the Digital Humanities', Digital Humanities Quarterly, 17(2). Introduction to the special issue
  • Fan, L.-T. (2023) 'Reverse Engineering the Gendered Design of Amazon's Alexa: Methods in Testing Closed-Source Code in Grey and Black Box Systems', Digital Humanities Quarterly, 17(2).
  • Vee, A. (2023) 'BASIC FTBALL and Computer Programming for All', Digital Humanities Quarterly, 17(2).
  • Cayley, J. (2023) 'Computational Art Explorations of Linguistic Possibility Spaces: Comparative Translingual Close Readings of Daniel C. Howe's Automatype and Radical of the Vertical Heart', Digital Humanities Quarterly, 17(2).
  • Whalen, Z. (2023) '"Any Means Necessary to Refuse Erasure by Algorithm": Lillian-Yvonne Bertram's Travesty Generator', Digital Humanities Quarterly, 17(2).
  • Boyd, J. (2023) 'Poetry as Code as Interactive Fiction: Engaging Multiple Text-Based Literacies in Scarlet Portrait Parlor', Digital Humanities Quarterly, 17(2).
  • Hua, M. and Raley, R. (2023) 'How to Do Things with Deep Learning Code', Digital Humanities Quarterly, 17(2).
  • Berry, D. M. (2023) 'Tracing "Toxicity" Through Code: Towards a Method of Explainability and Interpretability in Software', Digital Humanities Quarterly, 17(2). Reading toxicity through the source
  • Rountree, B. and Condee, W. (2023) 'Nonsense Code: A Nonmaterial Performance', Digital Humanities Quarterly, 17(2).
  • Corbett, J. (2023) 'Programming with Cree# and Ancestral Code: nêhiyawêwin Spirit Markings in an Artificial Brain', Digital Humanities Quarterly, 17(2).
  • Temkin, D. (2023) 'The Less Humble Programmer', Digital Humanities Quarterly, 17(2).
